Lumora

A hero you watch from a train

A full-screen hero for an invented mindfulness app, framed as a carriage window. Four films run past outside and the interface sits on the glass in front of them, so changing scene crossfades the world rather than the page. The bright scene turns the whole block of copy to ink, because white type has nothing to hold onto against snow.

Securify

A headline placed rather than typeset

A full-screen hero for an invented data-security platform. The headline is cut into three words and pinned to different corners of the frame, so the eye steps down the page and the film keeps showing through the gaps between them. Nothing sits on a panel: the type, the stats and the navigation all lie straight on the picture.

ClubOS

One app, every club's colours

A management app for grassroots football clubs, shown here as Firhouse Carmel FC. Fixtures, tables, squads, team selection and availability polls, all running on the club's real Amateur Football League season. The club is not hard coded: crest, colours, grounds and teams are data, so a club signs up, picks two colours, and the whole app arrives in their identity.

Why bother?

Because the fastest way to understand an interaction is to build it. Everything here started as a question: what does scroll feel like moving sideways, what makes a hover feel physical, how much can a single shader carry. The answers end up back in client work, which is the real point.
