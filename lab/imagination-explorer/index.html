The heart of the story

What the orb really is

The orb isn’t something Leo found. It’s something he carries. Leo is autistic. His senses are turned all the way up. Some days are overwhelming, some days are luminous, and many days are both. The same mind that feels this world so strongly is the one that builds whole worlds of its own.

The Imagination Explorer was made to help children, and the grown-ups beside them, see autism not as a puzzle to be solved but as a different way of being in the world. Not a story about what Leo can’t do. A story about everything he can.

Turned all the way up

Buzzing lights, scratchy labels, three people talking at once. Leo’s senses take everything in without a filter. The hush of his headphones isn’t hiding. It’s how he makes room for wonder.

The deep dive

When Leo loves something, he loves it completely: every fact, every detail, every drawing of it he can make. That focus isn’t a fixation to break. It’s the engine of every realm he builds.

Honest as sunlight

Leo says exactly what he means and means exactly what he says. In his worlds, and in ours, that isn’t rudeness. It’s a kind of truth most of us have to relearn.

Different, not less.
Leo doesn’t need fixing. He needs a world that wonders with him.

Inside the orb:
his worlds.

Every episode of The Imagination Explorer follows Leo into a realm his mind has built, each one shaped by how he senses, sorts, and celebrates the world.

“Every world he builds is a way of telling us how this one feels.”
